# CAIE IGCSE Additional Mathematics 0606: syllabus map (exams in 2025, 2026 and 2027)

This is a scope guide written in my own words. It is not the official text.
Version 2 (2026-10-10). Checked against the official Cambridge syllabus PDF for 2025-2027, read through a summarising tool, not word for word.
Anyone relying on this should check the "formulae given" list and the "not in the syllabus" list against the PDF.
Practice generator topic names are in square brackets, like [diff].

## Exam format
- Two written papers. Each is 2 hours and 80 marks, and each counts for 50%. All questions are compulsory.
- Paper 1 is non-calculator. Paper 2 needs a scientific calculator.
- Paper 1 means exact answers matter: surds, fractions, pi and ln. Mental arithmetic and tidy algebra count.
- Mark schemes give more marks for working than they used to. Method marks matter.
- Indices and surds are assumed knowledge from IGCSE Mathematics (0580). They are not a topic here, but many questions rely on them.

## Formulae given in the exam
The list given to candidates contains (from the syllabus):
- the equation of a circle with centre (a, b) and radius r
- curved surface area of a cone, surface area of a sphere, volume of a pyramid or cone, volume of a sphere
- the quadratic formula
- the binomial theorem
- arithmetic series: nth term and sum
- geometric series: nth term, sum, and sum to infinity
- the three Pythagorean identities (sin^2 + cos^2 = 1, sec^2 = 1 + tan^2, cosec^2 = 1 + cot^2)
- the sine rule, the cosine rule, and area of a triangle = (1/2) a b sin C

Everything else must be known. That includes all differentiation and integration results, the laws of logarithms, exact trig values, nCr and nPr, and the discriminant conditions.
Teaching rule: do not make the student memorise the given formulae, but they must know how and when to use them.

## Topics

### 1. Functions [func]
- Domain and range. One-one and many-one functions. Function notation.
- Composite functions and inverse functions. The graph of an inverse is a reflection in y = x. Explain why some functions have no inverse.
- The relationship between y = f(x) and y = |f(x)|, and sketching y = |f(x)| for linear, quadratic, cubic and trig f.

### 2. Quadratic functions [quad]
- Maximum and minimum values by completing the square or by calculus. Using them for sketches and for the range.
- The discriminant and the nature of the roots. Intersection of a line and a curve, and tangency.
- Solving quadratic equations and quadratic inequalities [ineq].

### 3. Factors of polynomials [factor]
- Remainder theorem and factor theorem. Finding factors. Solving cubic equations.

### 4. Equations, inequalities and graphs [mod] [ineq]
- Modulus equations: |ax + b| = c, |ax + b| = cx + d, |ax + b| = |cx + d|, |ax^2 + bx + c| = d.
- Modulus inequalities: k|ax + b| > c, k|ax + b| <= c, k|ax + b| <= |cx + d|, |ax + b| <= cx + d, |ax^2 + bx + c| > d, |ax^2 + bx + c| <= d.
- Substitution to turn an equation into a quadratic.
- Sketching cubics written as a product of three linear factors, and their moduli. Solving cubic inequalities with a graph.

### 5. Simultaneous equations [simul]
- Two unknowns, including one linear and one non-linear equation, by substitution or elimination.

### 6. Logarithmic and exponential functions [log]
- Graphs and asymptotes of e^x and ln x.
- Laws of logarithms and change of base. Solving a^x = b and equations involving logarithms.

### 7. Straight-line graphs [line] [lin]
- Equation of a line, parallel and perpendicular lines, midpoint, length of a segment, perpendicular bisector.
- Linear law: change y = A x^n and y = A b^x into straight-line form, then find the constants from the gradient and intercept. Also the reverse, for example a straight line of y^2 against x^3.

### 8. Coordinate geometry of the circle [cgeo] (new for 2025-2027)
- The equation of a circle. Intersections of a line and a circle, and of two circles.
- Tangents and chords. Common chords.

### 9. Circular measure [circ]
- Radians. Arc length and sector area.
- Problems that combine sectors with triangles are standard practice for this topic (check the PDF for the exact depth).

### 10. Trigonometry [trig] [tgraph]
- The six functions: sin, cos, tan, sec, cosec, cot. Exact values for standard angles.
- Graphs of a sin bx + c, a cos bx + c, a tan bx + c. Amplitude and period.
- The three Pythagorean identities. Solving trig equations in a given range. Proving identities.
- Angles in degrees or radians as the question says. Calculus uses radians.

### 11. Permutations and combinations [perm]
- Telling them apart. n!, nPr and nCr. Arrangement and selection problems, with and without restrictions.

### 12. Series [binom] [prog]
- Binomial expansion of (a + b)^n for positive integer n, and finding a general term (for example, the term independent of x).
- Arithmetic and geometric progressions: nth term and sum of n terms.
- Sum to infinity of a geometric progression, and the condition for it to converge. Be able to explain why a progression does or does not have a sum to infinity.
- Contextual problems.

### 13. Vectors in two dimensions [vec]
- Notation: column vectors, i and j form, AB form, single letters.
- Position vectors, unit vectors, magnitude. Add, subtract, multiply by a scalar. Equate like vectors.
- Vector geometry problems, with a diagram supplied for harder ones.
- Combine and resolve velocities. Use a velocity vector to find a position. Contextual problems such as two particles colliding.

### 14. Calculus [diff] [tangent] [stat] [rates] [integ] [area] [kin]
Differentiation
- An informal idea of a limit and the derivative. Notation f'(x), f''(x), dy/dx, d2y/dx2, and small increments.
- Derivatives of x^n (any rational n), sin x, cos x, tan x, e^x, ln x. Constant multiples, sums, chain rule, product rule, quotient rule.
- Tangents and normals. Stationary points, with the first or second derivative test to justify maximum or minimum.
- Connected rates of change. Small increments and approximations. Practical maximum and minimum problems.
Integration
- Reverse of differentiation, with a constant of integration.
- Powers of x including 1/x, (ax + b)^n for any rational n (including n = -1), sin(ax + b), cos(ax + b), sec^2(ax + b), e^(ax + b).
- Definite integrals. Area between a line and a curve, between two curves, and sums of areas.
Kinematics
- Straight-line motion: displacement, velocity, acceleration, with constant or variable acceleration.
- Displacement-time, distance-time, velocity-time, speed-time and acceleration-time graphs.

## Not in the syllabus (do not teach as examinable)
- Addition formulae for sin(A + B) and cos(A + B), double angle formulae, and R sin(x + alpha).
- Derivatives of sec x, cosec x and cot x.
- Differentiation from first principles. Points of inflexion.
- Binomial expansion with negative or fractional n. Greatest-term problems.
- Matrices and set notation are not mentioned in the 2025-2027 syllabus.
If the student asks about one of these, say it is outside 0606 and not examined, and offer an examinable topic instead.

## Command words to teach
find, show that, hence, express, solve, sketch, prove, determine, calculate, explain
- "Hence" means use the previous result.
- "Show that" means the answer is given, so every step must be justified.
